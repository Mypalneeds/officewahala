Nigerian office slang glossary

A friendly glossary of the Nigerian office words, Pidgin and Lagos street slang you'll meet in Office Wahala, with meanings and examples.

Most asked: Oga means boss. Wahala means trouble or stress. "Do the needful" means do what's necessary. "Revert" means reply. "NEPA took light" means the power went off. A query is a formal warning letter from HR.

AbegPidgin
Please. Used to soften a request or plead.“Abeg, let me breathe!”In the game: A colleague's line in the game.
AgbadaYoruba
A flowing wide-sleeved robe worn by men over a shirt and trousers, often for big occasions.“He wore agbada to the end-of-year party.”In the game: Unlocks with the Big boss energy badge.
AgberoYoruba / Lagos
A tout at bus parks and roadsides who collects fees from drivers, often aggressively.“The agbero collected money before the bus could move.”In the game: Agberos walk across the road on the morning commute.
AnkaraNigerian English
Colourful wax-print fabric used for clothes. Many offices allow Ankara on Fridays.“It's Friday, wear your Ankara.”In the game: The Ankara Friday outfit unlocks with the TGIF badge.
BRTLagos
Bus Rapid Transit: Lagos buses with their own dedicated lanes. Other vehicles aren't supposed to use them.“Don't drive in the BRT lane.”In the game: The commute road has a BRT lane.
ComotPidgin
Leave, move out of the way, or take something out.“Comot for road!”In the game: The MD's driver shouts it in Pidgin mode.
DanfoLagos
The yellow minibuses with black stripes that carry passengers around Lagos.“I took a danfo to Obalende.”In the game: Danfos are the big obstacles on the commute.
Do the needfulIndian and Nigerian English
Do whatever is necessary. A classic line in office emails and memos.“Please do the needful and revert.”In the game: Oga says it when he chases you.
FilaYoruba
A soft cap worn by men, often with traditional outfits.“He tilted his fila to the side.”In the game: Part of the Agbada & fila outfit.
GeleYoruba
A tall, stiff head tie worn by women, especially for ceremonies.“Her gele was so big nobody behind her could see.”In the game: Aunty from Accounts and the Gele & iro outfit both wear one.
Gen / generatorNigerian English
A petrol or diesel generator used when the public power supply fails.“Put on the gen, NEPA has taken light.”In the game: Generator fuel gives you speed and light during blackouts.
IroYoruba
A wrapper skirt tied around the waist, worn with a blouse (buba) and gele.“She wore iro and buba to church.”In the game: Part of the Gele & iro outfit.
JollofWest African
Rice cooked in a spicy tomato and pepper base. Nigerians will argue about it with Ghanaians all day.“Who ate my jollof in the office fridge?”In the game: The lunch pack pickup is jollof with plantain and chicken.
Keke (Keke Napep)Nigerian English
A three-wheeled motorised taxi. 'Napep' comes from NAPEP, the National Poverty Eradication Programme, which introduced many of them.“Let's take keke to the junction.”In the game: Kekes are commute obstacles.
Na waPidgin
An expression of surprise, frustration or disbelief.“Na wa for this traffic o.”In the game: Used in Pidgin messages.
NEPA took lightNigerian English
The electricity has gone off. NEPA (National Electric Power Authority) was the old state power company; it was replaced in 2005, but people still use the name.“NEPA took light in the middle of the match.”In the game: From level 3, the office goes dark during NEPA blackouts.
OgaNigerian English (from Yoruba)
Boss, master or person in charge. Also used respectfully for any man in authority.“Oga is coming, look busy!”In the game: The main boss of the game.
OkadaNigerian English
A motorcycle taxi. The name is widely said to come from the old Okada Air airline.“Take okada if you want to beat the traffic.”In the game: Fast obstacles on the commute.
Owo da?Yoruba
'Where is the money?' A classic agbero line to drivers.“Owo da? Bring my own!”In the game: Agberos say it on the commute.
Per my last emailOffice English
A polite-sounding way of saying 'I already told you this'.“Per my last email, the report was due yesterday.”In the game: One of the office lines in the game.
Puff-puffNigerian English
Sweet fried dough balls, a favourite snack at parties and in offices.“Who brought puff-puff?”In the game: The Puff-puff power-up stops colleagues chasing you.
QueryNigerian office English
A formal letter asking an employee to explain a mistake or misconduct. Getting one is serious.“HR gave him a query for lateness.”In the game: The HR lady throws query letters at you.
RevertNigerian office English
Reply or get back to someone (not 'go back to an earlier state').“Kindly revert ASAP.”In the game: 'Please revert ASAP' appears on URGENT tasks.
Salary has enteredNigerian English
Your salary has arrived in your bank account, usually announced by a bank alert text.“Salary has entered! Time to pay debts.”In the game: Shown when you grab a salary pickup.
SenatorNigerian fashion
A plain, long-sleeved kaftan-style outfit for men, named after politicians who popularised it.“He wears senator every Friday.”In the game: Unlocks after you outrun Oga 10 times.
Sharp sharpPidgin
Quickly, right now.“Do am sharp sharp.”In the game: Used in Pidgin mode.
Shine your eyePidgin
Be alert, watch out.“Shine your eye for road.”In the game: Said about okadas in Pidgin mode.
Soft copyNigerian English
A digital copy of a document (the printed one is the 'hard copy').“Send me the soft copy.”In the game: An office line in the game.
TGIFEnglish
'Thank God it's Friday'. Celebrated loudly in Nigerian offices.“TGIF! Who's coming out tonight?”In the game: The rare Friday bonus pickup.
Up NEPA!Nigerian English
The cheer when electricity comes back after an outage.“Up NEPA! Light don come.”In the game: Shown when the blackout ends.
WahalaNigerian Pidgin (from Hausa)
Trouble, problems, stress, drama.“No wahala, I'll do it.”In the game: The whole game is about surviving it.
We are a family hereOffice English
What managers say, usually right before asking for unpaid overtime.“We are a family here, so stay till 9pm.”In the game: An office line in the game.
