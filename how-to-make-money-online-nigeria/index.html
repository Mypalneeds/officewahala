How to make money online in Nigeria

There is real money online for Nigerians, and a lot of noise. This guide sticks to methods that work, how long they take, and the traps in between.

By Mmadubuko Micheal, Analaize Limited, Lagos · Updated 2 November 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: The most reliable ways to make money online in Nigeria are to sell a skill (writing, design, coding, video editing, virtual assistance, tutoring), sell products or services through WhatsApp, Instagram and online stores, and land a remote job. Content creation and affiliate marketing can pay but take longer. Anything that pays you for doing almost nothing, or asks for a deposit first, is the fastest way to lose money online.

Key facts

  • Skills pay; schemes don't. Almost every reliable method is selling a skill or a product.
  • Expect weeks to months before the first steady income.
  • Online income is taxable in Nigeria like any other income.
  • Nigeria's SEC warned again in May 2026 about Ponzi schemes promoted on social media.
  • Free game prizes, like Office Wahala's Weekly Cup, are a fun extra, not an income.

Skills that sell well online

  • Writing: website copy, product descriptions, social media posts, CVs.
  • Design: flyers, logos, social media graphics, presentation decks.
  • Tech: websites, WordPress, spreadsheets, data cleaning, basic automation.
  • Video: short-form editing for brands and creators.
  • Admin: inbox and calendar management, research, data entry.
  • Teaching: WAEC/JAMB subjects, languages, coding for kids.

Pick one, learn it well from free resources, make three sample pieces, and show them to real clients.

Getting paid from abroad

Clients outside Nigeria usually pay through a freelance platform, a domiciliary (dollar) account, or an international payment service. Before you choose one, check its fees, how fast it converts to naira, and that it works with Nigerian banks. Never use someone else's account to receive money: it can get your bank account frozen.

What to avoid

  • "Invest ₦20,000, earn ₦5,000 daily" schemes, including ones dressed as trading bots or AI.
  • Task apps that pay for likes and follows, then ask for a deposit.
  • Courses that promise guaranteed income and sell mainly to their own students.
  • Anyone who needs your BVN, PIN or OTP.

Tax on online income

Money you earn online is income like any other, and Nigeria's tax rules were updated from January 2026. Keep simple records of what you earn and ask a tax adviser or your state internal revenue service what applies to you.

Your first 30 days

  1. Week 1: pick one skill. Choose a skill people already pay for and that you enjoy. Use free videos and guides to learn the basics.
  2. Week 2: make samples. Create three examples of your work. Do one free job for a small business you know in exchange for a review.
  3. Week 3: put it where buyers are. Set up a simple profile on a freelance platform, LinkedIn and your WhatsApp status with your samples and prices.
  4. Week 4: pitch every day. Send five short, personal messages a day to businesses that need your skill. Follow up once.
  5. Keep going: reinvest. Use your first earnings for data, tools or a course that raises your rates.

Questions people ask

What is the fastest way to make money online in Nigeria?

Selling a service you can already do (like design, writing, typing or tutoring) to people you know, through WhatsApp and Instagram. It is not instant, but it is real.

Can I make money online in Nigeria without paying anything?

Yes. Freelancing, tutoring and many remote jobs need only a phone or laptop, data and a skill. Be wary of anything that asks you to pay to start earning.

Is it legit to make money online in Nigeria?

Yes, millions do through freelancing, remote work and online selling. The illegitimate part is the schemes promising easy returns.

How much can I make online in Nigeria?

It varies widely by skill, effort and clients. Be suspicious of anyone quoting fixed daily earnings.

Can I make money playing games online in Nigeria?

You can win prizes in free contests such as the Office Wahala Weekly Cup, but treat that as a bonus, not an income.
