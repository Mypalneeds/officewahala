How to make money playing games in Nigeria

Yes, you can win real money playing games in Nigeria, but not the way most adverts say. Here is what actually pays, what is a trap, and where Office Wahala fits.

By Mmadubuko Micheal, Analaize Limited, Lagos · Updated 2 November 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: The honest ways to get paid for playing games in Nigeria are free-entry tournaments with real prizes (like the Office Wahala Weekly Cup), esports competitions, streaming or making videos about games, and paid game testing. Apps that pay you "per game", play-to-earn tokens and anything that asks for a deposit first are where most people lose money. Treat game prizes as a bonus for being good, not as a salary.

Key facts

  • Only the top players in any tournament win, so most players earn nothing. That is normal.
  • A legitimate prize game never asks you to pay, deposit or "activate" your account first.
  • Betting and lotteries are regulated in Nigeria; since a November 2024 Supreme Court ruling, mostly by each state.
  • Office Wahala Weekly Cups are free, skill-based and publish every winner and payment status on a public page.
  • In April 2025 the CBEX "double your money" platform collapsed with an estimated ₦1.3 trillion in losses.

Five ways games can really pay

Every real way of earning from games has one thing in common: someone is paying for something valuable. A sponsor pays for attention, a viewer pays for entertainment, a studio pays for feedback. If you cannot see who is paying and why, be careful.

What usually does not pay

These are the offers that fill WhatsApp statuses and TikTok comments. Some are outright scams; others simply pay so little that your data costs more than you earn.

  • "Earn ₦5,000 per game" apps. They show a growing balance, then ask you to reach an impossible minimum, watch endless ads or pay a "withdrawal fee".
  • Play-to-earn crypto games. Rewards are paid in tokens whose price depends on new players joining. When new money stops, the token price falls. Read our guide to play-to-earn games.
  • "Invest and play" platforms. If you must deposit before you can earn, it is not a game, it is an investment, and Nigeria's SEC keeps warning about unregistered ones.
  • Selling in-game money. Many games, including Office Wahala, forbid it, and buyers often pay with stolen cards.

Is it legal to win money from games in Nigeria?

Winning a prize for skill in a free contest is not the same as gambling. Gambling usually means paying to enter (a stake) for a chance to win. Since the Supreme Court ruling of 22 November 2024, lotteries and gaming are regulated mainly by each state (and by the federal government in the FCT), so rules can differ from state to state. If a game asks you to pay a stake, check that the operator is licensed where you live. This page is general information, not legal advice. See skill games vs betting for more.

How to start, safely

  1. Pick a free-entry contest. Choose tournaments that never ask for a deposit, like the Office Wahala Weekly Cup.
  2. Read the rules. Check who runs it, when it ends, how winners are checked and how prizes are paid.
  3. Check past winners. A real organiser shows past winners and whether they were paid. Office Wahala publishes them on the winners page.
  4. Practise. Your best single run counts, so play a few normal shifts first to learn the patterns.
  5. Protect your details. Never share your BVN, card PIN or OTP to "receive" a prize. Office Wahala winners only send a claim code and how they want to be paid.

Questions people ask

Can you really make money playing games in Nigeria?

Yes, but only in a few honest ways: winning free tournaments, esports, streaming, game testing or making games. Most people who play will not earn anything, and that is fine if the game is free and fun.

Which game pays the most in Nigeria?

There is no single answer. Esports tournaments pay the most to the very best players. For ordinary players, free weekly tournaments with small prizes, such as airtime or bank transfers, are the most realistic.

Do I need to pay anything to play the Office Wahala Weekly Cup?

No. Entry is free, there is no sign-up and nothing to buy. The game runs in your phone's browser.

Can I cash out the naira I earn inside Office Wahala?

No. The in-game naira is pretend money for the shop and outfits. Real prizes come only from the Weekly Cups and weekly leaderboards.

How do I know a game that pays is not a scam?

Real ones never ask for money first, explain exactly how winners are chosen, show past winners, and pay on time. See our list of red flags.
