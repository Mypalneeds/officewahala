Are play-to-earn crypto games legit in Nigeria?

Play-to-earn games promised to turn gaming into income. For most players, the maths never worked.

By Mmadubuko Micheal, Analaize Limited, Lagos · Updated 2 November 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer: Some play-to-earn games are real products, but they are high-risk for players. Rewards are usually paid in a token whose value depends on new players buying in. When new money slows, the token price falls and so do earnings. Many need an upfront purchase of characters or tokens, which makes them closer to a speculative investment than a game. If you want game prizes without that risk, choose free-entry tournaments.

Key facts

  • Most P2E games require buying an NFT, character or token to start.
  • Earnings are in tokens whose price can fall sharply.
  • Platforms promising guaranteed crypto returns match the SEC's Ponzi warning signs.
  • CBEX, a crypto platform popular in Nigeria, froze withdrawals in April 2025.

How play-to-earn actually pays

In most P2E games you buy a starter character or token, play to earn more tokens, then sell them for naira. The tokens only have value while someone wants to buy them. In the early months, new players buy in and prices rise; later, sellers outnumber buyers and prices fall. Late joiners often never earn back what they paid to start.

Warning signs in a P2E game

  • You must buy something before you can earn anything.
  • Earnings are quoted per day or per month, as if guaranteed.
  • Referral bonuses are bigger than gameplay rewards.
  • Withdrawals have waiting periods, minimums or fees that keep changing.
  • The team is anonymous and the "game" is barely playable.

What CBEX taught Nigerian investors

In April 2025 the CBEX trading platform, which promised to double users' money, stopped withdrawals. The EFCC estimated losses of about ₦1.3 trillion and opened an investigation with Interpol. CBEX was not a game, but the pattern (fast "returns", referral rewards, sudden freeze) is the same one to watch for in any earn-while-you-play platform.

Questions people ask

Is crypto gaming legal in Nigeria?

Playing games is legal. Platforms that take investments must follow Nigerian securities rules, and the SEC warns against unregistered ones. This is general information, not legal advice.

Can I make money from P2E without investing?

Some offer free starter accounts, but earnings are usually tiny and paid in tokens that can fall in value.

What is a safer alternative?

Free-entry tournaments with cash, airtime or voucher prizes, where you never buy anything.
